# Stage 1: Coffee Can Screening

1. Revenue Growth $\ge 10\%$ CAGR every year
2. Return on Capital: $ROCE / ROE$ $\ge 15\%$ every year
3. Leader in a niche: deep economic moat
4. Negligible debt and clean accounting

# Stage 2: Valuation Screening

1. Historical Relative $P/E$: Current $P/E < 1.25 \times \text{Historical Median P/E}$
2. PEG Ratio: Target a **PEG $\le 2.0x$ to $2.5x$** for proven wide-moat compounders.
	- Formula $$\text{PEG} = \frac{\text{Current P/E}}{\text{Forward 3-Yr Expected Earnings CAGR}}$$
3. Reverse DCF: Implied Free Cash Flow growth rate the current stock price demands over the next 10 years. The industry and the business model should have that growth potential feasible.

# Stage 3: Promoter Screening

## Promoter Alignment

1. **Holding Band:** **$> 45\%-50\%$** promoter/founder holding (or significant institutional holding where no individual promoter exists, such as in professional board-run firms like L&T or ITC)
2. **Zero or Minimal Pledging:** Promoter pledge must strictly be **$0\%$**
3. Pattern of Insider Selling during bull runs
4. Promoter/executive salary growth scales proportionally with net profit growth, ideally remaining under $5\%$ of net profits

## Capital Allocation

1. Management should redeploy cash back into high-ROCE core business lines ($> 18\%$). No hoarding of unproductive cash.
2. Empire Building should be on logical grounds: Flag any use of high-margin cash flows from the core franchise to fund unrelated, low-return acquisitions or speculative ventures.
3. **Value-Accretive Share Buybacks:** Top-tier management executes share buybacks primarily when the stock is undervalued, rather than issuing stock options (ESOPs) that dilute shareholders.

## Candor

1. **Transcript Candor:** Review quarterly earnings calls. Look for management that openly admits operational mistakes, discusses long-term capacity building over quarterly earnings guidance, and avoids blaming macro factors for company-specific misses.
2. **Conservative Guidance:** Prefer leadership that consistently under-promises and over-delivers.

## Succession

1. **Key-Person Risk** 
	- Is the company overly dependent on a single charismatic founder
	- The moat should be in the product or the business rather than in the network (like contacts with ruling party)
	- Promoters holding active political offices, contracts without open tenders
	- Promoter facing criminal charges
	- Aggressive political donations
	- Related party shell entities with unrecorded cash siphoning or beyond reasonable cash transfers to the declared use case
2. **Board Independence:** Ensure the board includes reputable, vocal independent directors who actively question related-party transactions (RPTs).

### Resources
1. Annual Reports (Contingent Liabilities & Legal Disclosures)
2. Search the promoter and company names in **SEBI Enforcement Orders** and the **RBI Wilful Defaulters list**.
3. **eCourts Services / High Court Portals:** Search company and promoter names for pending criminal litigation or serious civil disputes.
4. **MDR (Ministry of Corporate Affairs / MCA21):** Check director disqualifications under Section 164(2) of the Companies Act.
5. If a promoter or direct family member is politically active or contested elections, check their mandatory **Form 26 affidavit**, which publicly lists all active FIRs, criminal cases, and asset declarations.
