Creation and Distribution are lifelong mates. When we create something and want it to be used, it requires distribution. Distribution is inescapable. Its as simple as you can't store the grain in silos, you have to take it to the market else it rots unused. 

Then why just not let it rot? I will take a share of the grain whenever I am hungry, let the rest rot.

Well, rotting does not mean the grains have zero significance, several organisms not humans will have feast of their lives in that silo. From nature's perspective, nothing will go useless, it finds a way to make everything useful. If not already, we might be getting plastic processing micro-organisms within centuries.

So for nature, nothing's useless. But for us, letting that grain rot was total useless no matter what corner we look it from. We could have solved hunger for 100 humans a day only if we could have taken it to the market. We could have earned some profits ourselves and used that to create more grain and solve hunger for more humans next time. We could simply built a nice home out of the profits for ourselves.

Why would a farmer let their grain rot?
1. The market is thousand miles away
2. The amount of grain is too much to carry
3. Both 1 and 2
4. The farmer did not want to solve any problems associated with creation of grains

1, 2, and 3 looks like an Accessibility Problem. 4 looks like the farmer lacks Empathy, if mean sure you did not want to earn anything out of your toil, but how can you let the grain rot while people are sleeping hungry.

The picture now changes if we bring Quality in this discussion. As of now we were assuming that the Quality of grain was good enough to take it to the market. But maybe it was not even worth taking that grain to the market!

When we say that the Quality was bad, we get a couple of questions,
1. Why would a farmer create something which is bad quality? Maybe their intention was to create good quality but they were not skilful enough. Or maybe quality was not part of the end goals, hence the grain was never built for quality.
2. Why would someone distribute a bad quality grain? Reasoning can be quite similar to above question, either a skill issue or distributing quality was never part of the goal.

So in this farmer grain example, the lose-lose scenario is pretty much either of the one that we wasted good grain or we distributed bad grain.

The reason of Loss for the above scenario is both lack of Skill and Goal
1. The farmer was not skilful enough to create quality grain and the distributor was not skilful enough to identify the bad quality
2. The farmer never had a goal to create quality grain, the distributor never had a goal to bring quality grain to the market
3. The farmer was skilled to create quality grain but the distributor was not skilful to identify the quality
4. The farmer had a goal to create quality but the distributor had no such goal to distribute quality

The lack of Skill and Goal in either parties making an exchange leads to the Market's Misery. Ensure healthy markets by auditing the Skills and Goals of every parties.
